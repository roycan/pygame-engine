## A. Print & bring

- [ ] SMMC card — **one per squad** (`mission_p1_squad_mission_management_card.md`)
- [ ] **Tokens:** 26 small counters per squad (beans, bottle caps, cut paper) — one per

      (one sheet of paper makes ~100).
- [ ] MoSCoW reference card on the projector (menu footer has it too)

## B. The arithmetic — verified, and worth showing on the projector

- Menu total: **2+3+3+2+2+3+2+2+3+2+4+4 = 32 tokens** across 12 features.
- Squad budget: **12 tokens** → **20 tokens must be cut, in writing.**
- The chase rules (**2**, feature 1) are Must — the value center needs them — leaving
  **10 tokens** for everything else. That pressure is the lesson; do not soften it.
- Effort tokens are **relative work estimates** — not hours, money, or measured effort.
  State this once, verbatim, from the menu footer.
- If a squad finishes under budget (< 12): they left value on the table — send them
  back for Shoulds. If over: cut more. If exactly 12: check the core is intact.

## C. The client script (you are the Gamekeeper)

**Opening (Phase 0):** read the brief in character. Co-signed by the Biology
Department — say that sentence; it is the cross-disciplinary anchor.

**Likely questions and your answers:**
- *"Can we split a 3-token feature into smaller pieces?"* → Tokens are estimates; any
  split needs Gamekeeper approval. Ask for it in writing on the card.
- *"Can we do 13 tokens if we work faster?"* → The budget is **fixed** for this
  simulation. In real projects you would negotiate time or resources instead — that is
  literally Version C's negotiation, so tell fast squads to save their argument.
- *"Can we skip the chase rules and just do charts?"* → No — that fails the value
  center. Charts without predator–prey dynamics is a different project.
- *"What does 'dangerous but fair'… sorry, wrong mission."* → Yes — and notice you
  asked about **fairness from evidence**. That habit is the whole course.

**Version C client change (do not announce early):** at the moment squads finish
cutting, announce: *"The Biology Department insists on **food patches** — a new
3-token feature. The deadline does not move."* Their three legal moves: cut 3 tokens
of scope (written), justify more time/resources (who approves?), or postpone with
documentation. Watch for the silent fourth option — quietly absorbing it and hoping.
That is the failure this negotiation exists to prevent.

## D. Classroom guidance

- **Phase 1 pace:** 12 features in 5 minutes means ~25 seconds per mark. Costs are
  pre-printed — nobody calculates, everyone judges. If a squad stalls on one feature
  for a minute, that feature is **Could**.
- **The debrief question (Phase 2 close):** *"Every squad cut differently — which cuts
  were wrong?"* Answer: **none, if the core survived and exclusions were written.**
  Different defensible cuts are the norm in real project management.
- **Roles are seats, not cells:** every shinobi argues in Phase 1; the Scope Master
  only *chairs* it. Nobody "owns" a card section alone.
- **Squads of two:** roles merge (Coordinator + Scope Master; Quartermaster doubles as
  Quality Scout). **Fours:** add the Quality Scout / Risk Analyst and expect a fourth
  milestone proposal.
- **The mission's known failure modes** (watch for them): squads keeping everything
  and "working harder" (the budget is fixed — that is the point) · squads cutting the
  core to fund toys (value-center violation) · exclusions with no reasons (a cut is a
  decision, not an omission).

## F. Project-management tools — now and later

- **Today:** the printed card + physical token board **are** the PM tools. Cutting and
  paying with real tokens makes the trade-off physical. No accounts, no tutorials.
- **For the real SG 16 project (after proposal approval):** the Annex C timeline
  belongs in a live tool — recommend **Google Sheets** (school accounts; the timeline
  template is already a table) or **GitHub Projects** (their squad repos already exist;
  milestones/columns map to their PR flow). Introduce when the full project plan is
  drafted — not during P-1.
- **Be explicit with students:** the paper exercise teaches the *thinking*; it does not
  replace the digital tool requirement — the SG expects them to acquire tools beyond
  the prescribed ones, which is exactly the Sheets/GitHub Projects step later.

## G. Oral presentation logistics — YOUR decision (do not assume)

SG 16 allots 45–50 minutes for presentations of ~33 squads with a per-member delivery
criterion — that is ~80 seconds per squad including critique: **not realistic as one
front-of-class sequence.** Options, all teacher decisions:
(a) **Parallel stations** — 3 squads present simultaneously at 3 corners; you rotate.
(b) **Representative pitches** — 90 seconds per squad, split across two meetings.
(c) **Gallery walk** — squads stand by their annexes; you circulate and score.
Choose and book the room layout before the presentation meeting; the 5-pt oral rubric's
per-member criterion works in all three.
